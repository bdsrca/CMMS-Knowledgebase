# China Manufacturing Context and 中国智造

## Why include this context

This repository is about a CMMS/EAM knowledge base, not about industrial policy. The China manufacturing context is useful because it shows the kind of operating environment where a governed knowledge layer becomes valuable:

- more connected assets;
- more digital inspection data;
- more production and maintenance systems;
- more procedures and compliance requirements;
- higher uptime expectations;
- larger installed bases across factories, grids, rail, buildings, process plants, and industrial parks.

## 中国制造2025 and 中国智造

`中国制造2025` is the official policy name. `中国智造` is a common industry phrase used to describe the move toward intelligent manufacturing.

For CMMS/EAM product design, the practical interpretation is:

> As manufacturing becomes more connected and intelligent, maintenance knowledge becomes harder to manage manually. The platform needs governed source content, retrieval, citations, and feedback loops.

## Mapping to CMMS/EAM knowledge needs

| Manufacturing direction | CMMS/EAM knowledge need |
| --- | --- |
| Smart factories | Equipment SOPs, PM rules, energy guidance, inspection data, production constraints |
| High-end equipment | Model-specific manuals, service bulletins, warranty rules, failure modes |
| Industrial internet | Source governance across IoT, MES/MOM, PLM, SCADA, and CMMS data |
| Green manufacturing | Energy procedures, carbon-related operating notes, efficiency playbooks |
| Safety-critical sectors | Conservative answers, citations, role controls, audit trails |
| Service-oriented manufacturing | After-sales procedures, field service notes, spare parts, customer-site knowledge |

## Public examples and CMMS/EAM takeaways

### Midea Building Technologies

Public materials describe an AI-empowered chiller lighthouse factory and smart O&M recommendations. For a CMMS/EAM knowledge base, this points to a practical need: link product manuals, equipment diagnostics, service knowledge, warranty rules, and energy performance guidance.

### SANY Group

SANY's No. 18 Factory is presented publicly as a highly intelligent heavy-industry factory. Heavy equipment is a strong fit for knowledge-base assisted maintenance because questions can span factory assets, field equipment, telematics, hydraulic systems, spare parts, and service playbooks.

### State Grid

Drone inspection and defect recognition show a common future pattern: inspection systems detect issues, but maintenance teams still need approved procedures, severity rules, safety constraints, and work-order guidance.

### CRRC

Rail O&M is safety-critical. Public CRRC materials mention intelligent O&M and lifecycle services. A knowledge base in this context should be conservative, citation-first, and role-aware.

### Huawei and COSMOPlat

Industrial connectivity, digital twins, and industrial internet platforms increase the amount of usable maintenance context. They also make governance more important because data comes from many systems.

### Sinopec and Baowu/Baosteel

Process and steel environments are asset-intensive. They depend on permits, safety rules, inspection findings, reliability plans, and shutdown procedures. A governed knowledge base can reduce friction while keeping source evidence visible.

## Product stance

The repository does not claim that any of these companies use this implementation. They are examples of industrial environments where next-generation CMMS/EAM knowledge systems make sense.
