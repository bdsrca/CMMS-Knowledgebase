# Real-World Examples Mapped to CMMS/EAM Knowledge Base Design

These examples are intentionally written as product-design scenarios, not as claims about a specific deployment.

## 1. Chiller maintenance and energy performance

A large chiller manufacturer may have product manuals, service bulletins, commissioning notes, energy optimization guidance, warranty rules, and field service feedback.

A CMMS/EAM knowledge base can help by connecting:

- equipment model and serial context;
- compressor and heat-exchanger troubleshooting guidance;
- maintenance rate history;
- warranty rules;
- service recommendations;
- energy performance notes.

Good user question:

> What should I check first when this chiller has abnormal compressor noise?

Good answer behavior:

- cite the compressor noise triage SOP;
- mention safety and vibration checks;
- suggest opening recent alarms and PM history;
- avoid making an automatic shutdown decision without the required evidence.

## 2. Heavy equipment factory + field service

Heavy equipment environments combine factory maintenance with field fleet data. A service question may involve machine model, telematics, part availability, local repair procedure, and warranty notes.

A knowledge base can help by grounding answers in:

- service manuals;
- fault-code explanations;
- parts substitution rules;
- field repair playbooks;
- factory quality notes;
- technician training material.

Good user question:

> This pump truck has a repeated hydraulic pressure fault. Which troubleshooting path should I follow?

Good answer behavior:

- cite the fault-code procedure;
- list checks in safe order;
- show required parts;
- route the user to the asset history and parts list.

## 3. Grid inspection and work-order creation

Drone inspection and defect recognition can identify asset issues. The next challenge is turning inspection output into maintainable work.

A knowledge base can map:

- defect labels;
- severity definitions;
- inspection standards;
- safety clearance rules;
- repair procedure;
- required crew skill;
- work-order priority guidance.

Good user question:

> The inspection system marked this image as a damaged insulator. What procedure applies?

Good answer behavior:

- cite the inspection standard;
- cite the repair procedure;
- show the risk category;
- recommend human review before creating or approving the work order.

## 4. Rail fleet lifecycle O&M

Rail maintenance is safety-critical. Answers should be conservative and traceable.

A knowledge base can help with:

- maintenance manuals;
- inspection checklists;
- lifecycle O&M notes;
- component service intervals;
- failure mode playbooks;
- regulatory and safety procedures.

Good user question:

> Which checklist applies to this door-system fault before the train returns to service?

Good answer behavior:

- cite the approved checklist;
- state uncertainty clearly;
- route the user to inspection history;
- avoid bypassing required signoff.

## 5. Process plant shutdown planning

Process plants depend on permits, safety rules, spare parts, equipment isolation, and schedule coordination.

A CMMS/EAM knowledge base can help by connecting:

- shutdown procedure;
- permit-to-work rules;
- isolation steps;
- spare-parts requirements;
- contractor instructions;
- critical path notes.

Good user question:

> What documents should be reviewed before replacing this pump seal during the shutdown window?

Good answer behavior:

- cite the pump seal replacement checklist;
- cite the permit and isolation procedure;
- list required parts and skills;
- suggest planner review instead of automatically changing the shutdown plan.

## 6. Steel production inspection and root cause

Online inspection systems can detect defects. Maintenance teams still need to connect defects to equipment condition, process settings, and root-cause playbooks.

A knowledge base can help by linking:

- surface defect descriptions;
- process equipment history;
- lubrication and alignment procedures;
- roll-change SOPs;
- quality inspection standards;
- root-cause investigation notes.

Good user question:

> This defect pattern appeared after the last roll change. Which maintenance checks should we review?

Good answer behavior:

- cite the inspection standard;
- cite the roll-change checklist;
- suggest reviewing equipment history;
- avoid asserting root cause without evidence.
